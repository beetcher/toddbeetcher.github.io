# Review notes: where I am least sure (judgment library, draft)

All content is original draft material awaiting your review. It is practice and discussion material, not a validated instrument. Start with the items below; the rest is more routine.

## Items where two thoughtful people might pick a different `strong` choice
These were already softened (the rival choice is `ambiguous` with an editor note), but they are the most contestable:
1. `j06_s_confide_lead`: keep a colleague's confidence versus telling your manager. The strong choice asks the colleague to share their timing; both rivals are `ambiguous`.
2. `j06_s_rounding_lead`: raise it privately first (strong) versus present accurate figures without warning (ambiguous). Some would say accuracy matters more than the private conversation.
3. `j06_s_invoice_early`: tell your manager (strong) versus contact the supplier yourself (ambiguous). Company rules vary on who handles this.
4. `j03_s_forecast_college` and `j03_s_forecast_adult`: the same question has a different strong answer at the two levels ("one dry day proves little" versus "judge across many forecasts"). Each is true; the "which is strong" depends on what you want to teach. You may want to make both strong in a choose-two form.
5. `j05_s_expand_lead`: strong is "look for a smaller, undoable version". It assumes that option exists; the two ambiguous choices (take it, decline) are both defensible.
6. `j07_s_vendor_lead`, `j05_s_hire_lead` and `j02_s_pilot_lead`: the strong choice is a middle path, which may hide a real preference for "act now" or "wait". Check that the ambiguous choices read as respectable.
7. `j08_s_wash_adult`: the strong choice (coincidence and memory) is right, but "keep a record" is marked ambiguous. A lighter reading would make it strong too.
8. `j02_s_attend_college` (choose two): "the instructor chose the policy" and "attendance cannot show care" are both hidden assumptions; the item depends on you agreeing they are the two the argument needs.

## Level labels
- Difficulty is my judgment of ambiguity and number of competing considerations. The expert items (about 14) are the most likely to feel one level too easy or too hard.
- The `team_lead_or_manager` audience is placed at intermediate, advanced and expert only, with no basic items. If managers also need basic practice, the general_adult items can serve (change `audiences`).

## Stale or unstable facts
- None of the items names a person, date, price, ranking or current event. Numbers inside items are invented ("1 in 20", "30-in-100", "9 out of 10") and are used only as teaching numbers.
- Check once that the arithmetic in the feedback is acceptable: for the flagged delivery (1 in 20 flagged, 1 in 200 damaged) "at most about 1 in 10 flagged are damaged"; for the filtered email (1 in 10 flagged, 1 in 500 scams) "most flagged are harmless"; "five heads in five flips about once in 32 tries".

## Neutrality and sensitivity
- No political, religious, partisan or culture-war scenario. Two items touch workplace honesty (rounding figures, an invoice error) and confidentiality; the norms used (do not mislead, keep confidences, tell people with authority) are common but not universal. Please read `j06` with that in mind.
- I avoided health, money-hardship and family scenarios on purpose. One item (breakfast and test scores) mentions a health-adjacent habit, only as a correlation example.
- The "teen" items are written at plain reading level. Reading level was checked by eye, not by a formula.

## Style risks
- Several feedback entries start with "Yes." for the strong choice. This is deliberate for learners but may feel repetitive; tell me if you prefer a different pattern.
- Some `incorrect` choices are intentionally mild (a sensible-sounding shortcut). A few may read as `ambiguous` to you; moving them is a one-field change on the set.
- Accidental clues: I checked that the strong single-choice answer is never more than 1.25 times the length of the next longest choice (a test enforces it), but wording clues are checked by eye only.

## Known gaps
- Thin pools: see COVERAGE.md (college intermediate and advanced, teen above introductory, manager basic, adult introductory and expert).
- Answer reuse is low compared with a fact library. Judgment answers are tied to scenarios, so reuse comes mostly from shared scenarios and a few general moves.
- Reasoning-error items name the error by function in the feedback. The classic names (circular reasoning, cherry-picking, false dilemma, appeal to authority, slippery slope) appear in the feedback, but "straw man", "ad hominem" and "false cause" are only described, not named, in a few places.
- No item uses an image, chart or link; items that ideally need a table (for example a base-rate table) are described in words.
