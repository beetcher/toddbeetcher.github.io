# Review notes: U.S. geography library (draft)

Written by the builder after a self-review as a subject-matter editor and a reading-level editor. These are the items I am least sure about, so you can start there. Nothing here has been checked by a geographer, a teacher or a reading-level tool. Everything is `draft`.

## 1. Names I left out on purpose
- The official name of the highest peak in North America has been changed by government order more than once. No question names it. The highest point in the 48 connected states (Mount Whitney, Sierra Nevada) is asked instead.
- The official name of the large body of water south of the Gulf Coast states has also been changed by order. No question or feedback names it. The Mississippi River is described by what it is, not by where it ends.
- No population, ranking of cities, or "fastest" claims. The only superlatives are: Alaska has the most land of the states; Lake Superior has the largest surface area of the Great Lakes; Lake Michigan is the only Great Lake wholly in the United States; Death Valley holds the lowest point; Mount Whitney is the highest point in the 48 connected states. I am confident of each, but please confirm them.
- I did not ask for the longest river in the country. Sources disagree depending on whether the Missouri and Mississippi are measured separately or together.

## 2. Facts that depend on a boundary or a rule that could change
These are stable now but are the first to go stale:
- Time zones: Texas split (El Paso area on Mountain time); western Florida Panhandle on Central; northern Idaho Panhandle on Pacific; most of Arizona not observing daylight saving time (the Navajo Nation does, and the feedback says "most"). Files 05 (`texas_two_zones`) and 06 (`panhandle_zone`, `idaho_panhandle`, `arizona_zone`). Boundaries are moved by federal rule from time to time.
- Territory list: Guam, American Samoa, Puerto Rico, the U.S. Virgin Islands (not asked) and the Northern Mariana Islands. "Commonwealth" for the Northern Mariana Islands, and "unincorporated" for American Samoa and Guam (file 05/06 only), are legal terms; please confirm the wording you want.
- Census regions: Ohio, Michigan, Iowa, Kansas, Nebraska in the Midwest; Delaware, Maryland, D.C., Georgia, Texas, Arkansas in the South; Montana, Idaho, Oregon in the West; Maine, Vermont, New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania in the Northeast. The Bureau has kept these fixed for a long time, but every region question names the convention in the stem.
- Washington, D.C. "is a federal district". Statehood proposals exist. No question states anything about representation or statehood; only that D.C. is not a state today and why a federal district exists.

## 3. Items where experts might legitimately disagree (marked `ambiguous`, 8 choices)
- Texas in the "Southwest" (sample), Pennsylvania as "Mid-Atlantic", Delaware as "Northeast", Missouri as "border state", Oklahoma and regional labels (two sets), "Father of waters" for Mississippi (two sets), Arizona as "Mountain time". Each has an editor note. I used `ambiguous` only where a respected usage exists, so the count is small. If you want more ambiguity practice, regions are the place to add it.
- The meaning of "Mississippi": "great river" is the direct Ojibwe meaning; "father of waters" is the popular translation. I marked the popular one ambiguous. Please confirm you are comfortable with that call.

## 4. Place-name origins (least sure of the wording)
- Boise from a French word for wooded (the usual account, but the story is partly folklore). Santa Fe "holy faith". Colorado "colored red" (I wrote "probably for the red rock and river"). Nevada "snow-covered". Montana "mountainous". Ohio from an Iroquoian word (good river or great river).
- I made the Boise and Santa Fe answers incorrect only for the question "named for a U.S. president". If the Boise origin worries you, replace Boise with another capital.

## 5. Possible accidental clues and weak items
- Several wrong-answer feedbacks name the correct answer ("The capital is Austin"). That is deliberate teaching, but if these sets are ever shown back to the learner before answering, they would give the answer away.
- `hemispheres` (grades 6 to 8): the stem says "north of the equator and west of the Prime Meridian", so it is mostly a vocabulary check. It is `basic` for that reason.
- `lat_long` and `climate_weather` test vocabulary more than reasoning. Fine for `basic`, but they are not deep.
- The cluster questions ("Which of these is a river / lake / desert / mountain chain?", "Which city is the capital of Colorado / Massachusetts?", the Census region quartet, the time conversion set) share answer choices on purpose so that atoms are reused with changed roles. They are simple. They exist to show reuse and for younger tests.
- `dc_capital` feedback for Los Angeles says "Try the city named for the first President". It is a mild hint and could be removed.
- Correct-answer position is shuffled by a fixed rule. I checked that the strong choice is never clearly the longest in a single-choice set (ratio test), but I did not measure position balance across the library.

## 6. Difficulty and reading level
- `difficulty` is relative to the band. A grades 3 to 5 `basic` set and an adult `basic` set are not equal. The audience matrix is in COVERAGE.md. Filter on audience first.
- Reading level was matched by hand (sentence length, vocabulary), not measured with a tool.
- Grades 6 to 8 has no `introductory` sets and grades 9 to 12 has none either. Introductory sets live in grades 3 to 5 and general adult. Add some if you want a younger test that starts lower for those bands.
- Expert sets exist only for the enthusiast band (7 sets). General adult has only 2 advanced sets.
- Time-zone arithmetic appears in several bands (each with different times and zone pairs). It is intentionally repeated at four reading levels, but it is the same skill.

## 7. Size
The plan asked for about 50 questions and at least 50 sets. The main files hold 102 questions and 109 sets, because the cluster additions and the cross-band placements added sets without adding many answers. Review effort is therefore about double what the plan assumed. If that is too much, the easiest cut is the cluster additions at the end of each main file (the last two to four sets of files 02 to 06, for example the sets whose question text starts "Which of these is a ...", "Which city is the capital of Colorado..." and the Census region quartet).

## 8. What I did not do
- No check by an independent source. All facts come from my own knowledge, restricted to well-established ones.
- No cross-check of reading level or answer-position balance across the whole library.
- Nothing was imported to the live service.
