## Intent
A skill-ladder catalog for smoking meat at home: from a person who has never lit a smoker to an experienced pitmaster. This is the demo of a hands-on, practical-skill certification pool, so questions should test judgment and technique in realistic situations, not trivia.

## Subtopics (use as topics/tags)
food safety and temperatures; equipment and fuel (offset, kamado, pellet, electric, kettle, charcoal and wood types); fire and airflow management; wood and smoke quality (clean vs. dirty smoke); meat selection and trimming; seasoning, brining and rubs; temperature targets and probe doneness; stalls, wrapping and timing; resting and holding; slicing and serving; troubleshooting (too bitter, dry, tough, uneven cooking, temperature swings, flare-ups); cleanup and safety around fire.

## Audiences (suggested)
`hobbyist_beginner`, `home_enthusiast`, `backyard_host_for_a_crowd`, `aspiring_competitor_or_caterer` (adjust in TAXONOMY and justify).

## Difficulty meaning (adapt in TAXONOMY)
introductory = first cook, basic safety and vocabulary; basic = follow a recipe reliably; intermediate = manage the fire and the meat independently and fix common problems; advanced = plan timing for a crowd, adapt to weather and equipment, diagnose subtle faults; expert = refine technique, teach others, reason about trade-offs.

## Category-specific content rules
- **Food safety is the one place with firm right answers.** Use only widely-agreed, stable guidance: the temperature danger zone and the two-hour rule, safe minimum internal temperatures from USDA food safety guidance, cross-contamination, thermometer use and placement, not leaving meat out, safe thawing and chilling, safe handling of leftovers. If you are not certain of an exact number, ask about the principle instead of the number. State safety facts conservatively and check each one twice in the self-review.
- **Technique is full of legitimate disagreement.** Wood choice, spritzing, wrapping (foil vs. butcher paper), the stall, hot-and-fast vs. low-and-slow, resting time, injecting, and "done by temperature vs. done by feel" have defensible schools of thought. Test the underlying reasoning; mark the debatable choices `ambiguous` and explain the trade-off in the feedback. Do not declare folk myths true or false unless you are certain (for example, test whether a person knows doneness is judged by probe feel and temperature together).
- No curing chemistry quantities, no nitrite or nitrate dosing, no instructions for canning, no raw-milk or similar high-risk topics. If a question touches curing, keep it to recognizing that cured products need exact, tested recipes and that guessing is unsafe.
- No brand names, no product recommendations, no prices. Fuel and equipment questions are about types and behavior.
- Scenarios are concrete (weather, timing a family dinner, a stall at hour six, a bitter bark) rather than definitions.
- Fire safety and carbon monoxide: include as safety judgment items (outdoor use only, distance from structures, never leave a fire unattended, ash disposal).
