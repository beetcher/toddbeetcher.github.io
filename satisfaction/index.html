A Crime With No Criminal:
Who Stole My Child's Satisfaction?

The answer isn't on your child's phone. It started before your child's parents met.

↓

Many young people are thriving. This is written for the growing population who aren't — and for the parents and leaders who sense something is wrong but cannot name it.

Humans are not born with identity or satisfaction. They build it through recognized contribution. That cycle produces identity, satisfaction, agency, and the motivation to face the unknown. Remove it and what fills the vacancy is malaise. Social media didn't create the vacancy. It moved in when we did.

There is no villain. Every washing machine, every kiosk, every delivery app, every robot fry station was a rational decision celebrated as progress. The fast food industry's 144 percent annual turnover wasn't a problem — it was the revolving door that guaranteed every teenager a first job. Miso Robotics has raised over $100 million to close that door forever. They do not ask what the teenager loses when the shift disappears. Neither does anyone else.

The consequence is not hunger or homelessness. It is a generation that cannot launch — hollowed out not by hardship but by the quiet removal of the only mechanism that builds the motivation to face the unknown.

Youth unemployment for ages 16 to 24 has risen for three consecutive years. The Bureau of Labor Statistics says the reasons are not clear.

They are clear. The full case is in the four documents below, best read in this order:

  1. 1 Are We Automating Away Our Children's Path to Satisfying Lives? Start here. The complete causal argument.
  2. 2 Why Nobody Fixed It The diagnostic. Why a villainless problem resists every institutional response.
  3. 3 The Disappearing First Job The commercial evidence. Company by company, category by category.
  4. 4 The Tractor and the Algorithm The AI implications. What invisible prerequisite knowledge looks like today.
