Creative review • October 27, 2026 • Local concepts only

## Recommendation

Keep Todd and his actual work at the center. Use real photographs, actual application screens and readable excerpts as evidence. Add a few warm editorial illustrations to explain ideas that a screenshot cannot. Give visitors a chance to try something before inviting a conversation.

The emotional sequence is: **I recognize this feeling → this person understands it → he has done real work → I can picture a useful first step → I would like to talk.**

## Make the site enjoyable through discovery

1. **Show one real thing per contribution.** A console screen, an annotated page, a photograph. Give it a short “What you're looking at / Why it matters” reveal. Keep the ordinary link available without requiring the reveal.
2. **Invite a tiny act of noticing.** “What has been harder than it needed to be today?” Let someone consider an everyday example. This makes the friction-recognition insight tangible.
3. **Connect surprising examples.** A brisket, a repair and a territory application can share an optional “same habit, different problem” thread. Show the actual work where possible. Avoid turning the whole page into an animation.
4. **Let visitors choose depth.** Clear, specific links such as “See the venue workflow” and “Read the learning paper” support a fast main read and deeper sideways exploration. Small optional audience jump links can help without replacing the common story.
5. **Add Todd's actual voice later.** A short, optional video about recognizing a moment of friction would demonstrate his manner and coaching style. Use a poster image, captions and transcript; no autoplay. Recording and final placement remain future work.
6. **Keep conversation easy.** A persistent, unobtrusive “Talk with Todd” control should remain available; the readiness activity is another entrance, never a prerequisite.

## Are You Ready: restore it as a first-class endpoint

The name can stay provocative while the welcome removes the test anxiety:

> **You don't have to be ready for everything. Let's find a useful next step.**

Offer a secondary entry near the hero and a full invitation at the end of the narrative. Keep employer-specific introductions compatible with the same core endpoint. Preserve employer context in a future conversation brief only when it is accurate and explicitly shown to the visitor.

### Local prototype provided

Open `index.html` through the local review server and choose “Try Are You Ready.” Five short questions ask:

1. Who are you thinking about: yourself, an organization, or a learning community?
2. Where are you starting with AI?
3. What most gets in the way?
4. What would make a next step worthwhile? Optionally name a task.
5. What kind of help would be useful?

The result provides three practical actions, a relevant artifact, and a copyable conversation brief. Four authored routes address beginning, recognizing friction, judgment, and useful work. Audience changes the introductory context; experience, goal and support appear in the brief. This first prototype does **not** yet use every answer to alter the advice, and should not be presented as deeply personalized assessment.

The prototype has no score, AI call, email gate, submission, analytics or saved answers. It works from authored content and page-memory state. Its conversation button previews a future handoff and sends nothing. Reloading clears answers.

### Why this belongs here

It lets the visitor experience the teaching approach: start with their own context, name a manageable problem, make a useful choice, and retain agency. It also gives Todd a better opening conversation than a generic “interested in AI” inquiry.
