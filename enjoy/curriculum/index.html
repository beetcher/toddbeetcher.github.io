What you’ll learn

Five building blocks, practiced on real, everyday moments.

It isn’t a course on how AI works. It’s a hands-on evening of trying it on the things you already do, so you leave with a few small wins and the habit of spotting the next one.

The first step

Think of it as helping you into the pool. I’m not here to swim every lap with you. I’m here to make sure you get in: that you can log in and use it on your phone and your computer, that you know how to ask for what you want, and that you can organize some basic capabilities. Where you go from there is entirely up to you.

If all you ever use it for is recipes and letters, that’s wonderful. What I want to remove are the things that stop people from starting: not knowing what to ask, not knowing how to get set up, and feeling like everyone else already gets it.

Block 1

Spot the moment

The real skill. While you’re driving, hosting, cooking, fixing or writing, you notice: this is a moment where AI could help. We practice the reflex until it’s automatic.

Block 2

Say what you want

How to ask in plain words, with enough detail to get something good. You’ll see the exact prompts that worked, and try a few yourself.

Block 3

Keep the conversation going

The first answer is rarely the last. How to nudge, change your mind and ask again, the way you would with a clever friend.

Block 4

Notice when it’s wrong

Everyone gets a miss. You’ll see a few of ours and learn to tell when to double-check, and when to call a professional instead.

Block 5

Every day, not all day

Make it a small, happy habit, not another screen to live in. The aim is more time away from the device and more real-life results.

The moments we practice on

  • Hosting
  • Driving
  • Cooking
  • Fixing
  • Writing to people you love
  • Getting out of a hassle

What to expect

Think book club, not classroom. A relaxed, small group where people share what they tried, what worked and what flopped. No grades, no homework, no one left behind.
