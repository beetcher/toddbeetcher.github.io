## Intent
Original study-practice questions covering the kinds of civics and American history knowledge that people prepare for when applying to become US citizens, plus general civic literacy for teachers and learners. This is practice material, **not the official naturalization test, not affiliated with or endorsed by USCIS, and not legal or immigration advice.** Say so in `TAXONOMY.md`, in the sample reading guide, and in each record's `origin.notes` (one short sentence).

## Audiences (suggested)
`naturalization_applicant`, `adult_english_learner` (very plain English, short sentences, no idioms), `civics_teacher_or_tutor`, `general_adult_learner`.

## Subtopics (use as topics/tags)
principles of American democracy (rule of law, separation of powers, checks and balances, the Constitution as supreme law); system of government (the three branches, Congress, the presidency in general terms, the courts, federalism and the states, elections and voting rights in general terms); rights and responsibilities (the Bill of Rights, freedoms, duties such as obeying laws, serving on a jury when called, paying taxes, and voting); colonial period and independence; the 1800s (expansion, the Civil War, slavery and its end, constitutional amendments); recent American history (world wars, civil rights movement, the Cold War) at a stable, well-agreed level; symbols and holidays (the flag, the anthem, national holidays); geography at a civics level; reading and writing practice items (vocabulary-in-context questions, not audio dictation).

## Category-specific content rules
- **Never write questions whose correct answer depends on who currently holds an office or on any recent change** (president, vice president, senators, representatives, governors, the Speaker, Supreme Court membership, current party control, recent laws or rulings). The official test's answers for such items vary by person and place and go stale; exclude them. If you want to test how an office works, ask about the office, not the officeholder.
- **Do not reproduce the official USCIS question list or answer wording.** Write original questions on the same topics. Do not claim to know which version of the official test is currently in effect or its exact structure; add a line in REVIEW-NOTES for Todd: "check the current official USCIS civics test materials before using this for real applicants."
- Content that must be exactly right: treat each historical or constitutional fact as high-stakes. Use only facts you are certain are stable and standard (constitutional structure, amendment topics, major historical events and their well-established order). If scholars legitimately differ on interpretation, mark ambiguous.
- Handle slavery, Native American history, immigration and civil rights accurately, plainly and without partisan framing. No political-party questions beyond neutral definitions.
- Levels: introductory = plain recall of a basic fact in simple English; basic = standard study-guide level; intermediate = connecting ideas (why a branch has a power); advanced = applying a principle to a new situation; expert = nuanced civics reasoning for tutors and teachers.
- For `adult_english_learner`, keep every question and answer short, avoid idioms, define uncommon words inside the question when needed, and make feedback gentle and clear.
