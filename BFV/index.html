THE OPPORTUNITY

02 / A thought experiment · IBM Rochester, 1989

Ten minutes of coding.
Weeks of waiting.

Imagine a summer intern with a tool as capable as today’s AI coding assistants. He finishes his assignment in minutes.

The code still has to pass through meetings, reviews, integration, testing, and release.

The programmer has moved to machine time. The organization hasn’t.

An imagined scenario, not a historical account. The illustration’s events and timing are fictional.

03 / Same experiment · Everyone gets the tool

Faster programmers.
The same bottleneck.

Now give the tool to every programmer. Code generation accelerates across the company.

Coordination, dependencies, integration, testing, and approvals still take human time.

A coding productivity gain can leave the delivery constraint untouched.

05 / Two markets · One factory

Different starting points.
A shared production opportunity.

A / TRANSFORM AN EXISTING CAPABILITY

“We already spend heavily on software.”

Investigate whether a different manufacturing model can reduce production cost while preserving essential capabilities and continuity.

The potential implications reach operating expenses, organizational design, and enterprise value.

B / ACCESS A NEW CAPABILITY

“We could never justify building it.”

Explore applications, automation, and proprietary digital capabilities that may become practical with lower production cost and organizational burden.

The proposition is affordable access to capabilities the enterprise wants.

The uncomfortable part

Transforming an existing production organization can change roles, departments, and management structures. The customer decides staffing and restructuring. Black Flag’s role is to supply a credible manufacturing alternative.

07 / The commercial relationship

Partner with the enterprise.
Leave ownership with its owners.

Black Flag proposes to participate commercially in the transformation by supplying software manufacturing.

THE ENTERPRISE

Retains the business.

Its customers, brand, ownership, and leadership decisions remain its own. It captures the resulting customer-side economic improvements.

BLACK FLAG DESIGN

Earns manufacturing revenue.

BFD earns fees for delivering capability. Revenue must cover its costs and reserves before becoming available profit.

How this differs from acquisition

The paper contrasts Black Flag with acquisition-led operators such as Bending Spoons. Acquiring a company and supplying it commercially involve different capital requirements, ownership economics, and risks. Neither is universally superior.

Commercial pricing and the engagement structure remain to be established.

08 / Why Black Flag intends to move early

The harvest has two clocks.

01 / The factory advantage

Comparable manufacturing capabilities become more accessible. Competition increases and pricing faces pressure.

02 / The initial enterprise harvest

Enterprises transform, independently or with partners. The same untouched legacy cost structure cannot be transformed for the first time twice.

Both durations are uncertain. Ongoing service relationships may continue after the initial transformation.

And an agreement.

Potential profit remaining after those obligations.

04
Potential BFV capital

An agreed portion could support venture creation through a negotiated arrangement.

The BFD → BFV step is conditional. Amount, timing, mechanism, ownership, and governance remain unresolved. Additional capital may be needed.

Why the orchard arrows need this explanation

The orchard is a useful overview. Its arrows do not represent customer savings flowing directly into a fund. Customer savings, BFD revenue, available BFD profit, and BFV capital are separate economic concepts.

10 / Black Flag Ventures · Proposed venture engine

Plant with evidence.
Commit with judgment.

Combine capital, Business Design, factory access, and intelligent systems to create and own interests in new AI-native businesses.

01 / QUALIFY

Find a credible opportunity.

Understand the customer, market, competition, economics, and capital requirements.

02 / VALIDATE

Test what must be true.

Use structured experiments, customer evidence, and affordable manufacturing to test demand and feasibility.

03 / GRADUATE

Advance what earns it.

Move the strongest opportunities toward independent operation, financing, combinations, or other appropriate outcomes.

Evidence may also justify redirecting, combining, or stopping an opportunity, subject to ownership rights and governance.

BFV is a proposed venture-creation system. Formation, capitalization, portfolio rights, and operating performance remain to be established.

11 / What could endure

The harvest is finite.
The ambition is not.

Each venture generates evidence about customers, markets, manufacturing costs, and outcomes. That knowledge could improve how subsequent opportunities are evaluated and developed.

Over time, the enduring assets could be ownership, relationships, accumulated intelligence, and capital-allocation judgment.

Lower software cost does not create demand. Commercial profit does not guarantee successful investments.
