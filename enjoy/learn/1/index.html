Class one: from the blank screen to something you send.

Everyone starts from the same line. After that, the path is yours.

The picture

First, you see what you’re actually talking to.

Before you open any app, I show you this. Companies took the world’s knowledge, in every language, and built it into a giant Hall of Records. Somebody stands at the front door who can go through all of it for you. That’s your assistant.

When we’re done, you’ll be able to explain this to your family.

It has a date

What’s in the hall was gathered on a certain day.

Think of it as a snapshot of the world’s knowledge, taken at one moment. The world kept moving, and the next snapshot is a couple of years away. So some of what looks like a mistake is just an old answer, given with total confidence.

The internet door

It can go and look, but often only if you ask.

I once lost two days on setup steps that were years out of date. Then I asked one question, “Are you sure this is the only way?” and it went to the internet and found the new way. A person would just go look. Your assistant often won’t, unless you say so.

Are you sure that’s the current way? Please check the internet for the latest and show me where you found it.

In class, we ask the same question both ways and compare the answers.

Who it should be

Tell it who to be and what you need.

Your assistant can take on a role: a master copywriter, a patient plumber, a pianist, even a famous storyteller like Steven Spielberg. It has the whole hall behind it. But you usually have to say which one you want, and what you need help with.

You are a master copywriter. I need a short, warm email to my daughter about her new job. Make it funny, not mushy.

Role, goal, tone. That’s most of it.

The leash

Some things it holds back. That’s the company, not the hall.

Ask it to be your lawyer or your doctor and you’ll get a wall of qualifications. The knowledge is in the hall. The company that built your assistant put limits on what it will say, and every company’s limits are different. Read past the caveats: you can still ask it to explain, lay out your options, or help you prepare good questions.

Separate point: a confident answer can still be wrong.

When it pushes back

It will annoy you. That’s normal.

At some point it will correct you, talk down to you, or ignore what you asked. You may even swear at it. Remember: it’s making its best guess. It’s not out to get you, and it doesn’t know everything about you. Say it again with the role and the goal, or tell it the tone you want.

Be direct. Skip the lecture. Just answer.

Your judgment stays yours

Never hand off your judgment.

Ask for a summary and it picks what it thinks matters. For something casual, that’s great; have it read to you on the drive home. If it matters, a summary can leave out the one thing you needed. I’ve seen a meeting summary leave out a decision that was on the full recording. So you steer it:

Before you summarize, list every separate point this document makes, numbered. Then give me the summary and tell me which points you left out.

Then you read it yourself. Always.

Set it up and name it

One app, one account, and a name of your own.

We all start on the same free app, on your phone and in the browser, so you see the same conversation in both. I’ll show you how to spot the real one and the lookalikes to skip. Then you give your assistant a name. I call mine Doc. What will you call yours?

Talk like a person

Ramble. It pulls it together.

Your words can stop and start. Think of a text to a friend, not a search box. Type it or say it out loud. Then steer: shorter, in Spanish, funnier, warmer. You’re in charge of the result.

Make something real

Write something for someone you love.

Pick the person and how you want them to feel. Don’t write it word for word. Let it draft, read it yourself, say what’s off, and have it try again. Ask for three jokes and pick the one that sounds like you. That’s the whole skill: steer, read, redirect.

It becomes yours when you’ve made it yours.

Finish it

Add a picture, then hit send.

Have your assistant write a picture prompt, in the spirit of a favorite creator, and carry it to an image tool. Then put the picture and your words together in your own email and send it to someone you care about. Before you leave, it’s out the door.

What you take home

An assistant with a name, and something you already sent.

You’ll be able to explain the Hall of Records to your family, ask your assistant to check the internet, ask it to list every point, and know when to trust it and when to double-check. I leave you a one-page cheat sheet. Use it every day, not all day.
