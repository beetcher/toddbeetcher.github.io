I built a console that lets an operator choose a territory, specify the kinds of places to find, and use AI to propose candidates. Reviewed venues become permanent inventory the operation can use.

That discovery workflow connects to a larger system for people, classes, instructors, attendance, and communications. The important part is how the pieces work together.

03 / RECOGNIZING THE MOMENTThe person stays in charge.

The next time
something gets
in your way.

Knowing AI exists is different from noticing when to reach for it.

A useful first experience is a beginning. The next step is recognizing the ordinary moment when help could make a difference: something confusing, repetitive, unfamiliar, or simply worth understanding.

That is the habit I want to help people develop. Choose something that matters to them. Help them make progress. Then help them recognize the next opportunity themselves.

The light had been broken for years.

Then I thought to ask.

I’d lived with a dead dome light in our Honda. One day I thought to describe the car and show the assembly to my AI assistant. I could start working out what I needed to understand.

The important change came before the answer: I recognized a problem I could now approach differently.

  1. 01

    Notice.

    What’s getting in the way? Is this a useful place to ask for help?

  2. 02

    Frame.

    Describe the goal, the situation, and what you don’t yet understand.

  3. 03

    Try.

    Take one manageable step toward something that matters.

  4. 04

    Check.

    Question the answer. Verify what matters. Keep your judgment.

  5. 05

    Return.

    Recognize the next opportunity, with greater independence.

A brisket for friends, a website, an unfamiliar piece of code: the setting changes. The willingness to learn can travel with you.

At work or school, that also means appropriate tools, clear information boundaries, and a person who remains responsible for the result. Sometimes the right choice is to leave AI out.

06 / STARTING HEREA beginning shaped around your organization.

First, I’d walk around
and meet people.

Before deciding what should change, I’d want to understand the people doing the work—or the learning.

I’d listen to what they’re trying to accomplish, where work gets stuck, what they already use, and what makes them curious or uneasy. I’d learn with the managers, educators, and technical partners who know the setting.

Early in the week, I’d show examples from inside and outside work, then help people establish a usable AI environment within your tools and information boundaries.

I’d focus on recognizing opportunities and experiencing a useful first result. An end-of-week workshop could bring their questions and discoveries together and point toward useful applications in their own setting.

Then we’d select a few opportunities, agree on owners and what improvement would look like, and support people as they try again. We’d return to what got in the way, adjust the approach, and see what actually helps. The sequence would adapt to your organization; the first week is a launch, not a promise of transformation by Friday.

  1. CAPABILITY

    Can people recognize a suitable opportunity, explain what they need, try it, and check the result?

  2. CONTINUED USE

    Do they return on their own? Do they have usable examples, time to practice, and someone to ask when they get stuck?

  3. USEFUL WORK

    Does the chosen workflow improve—in quality, time, service, or another agreed measure?

A letter from Todd

AI arrived. I dove in.

Hi, I’m Todd. I’m glad you’re here.

I’m bringing decades in technology, business, and life into work I want to do: helping people and organizations find their way into the AI era.

When AI arrived, I wanted to understand what was underneath it. Then I dove in and kept swimming—building things, asking questions, getting stuck, and learning how to steer. As I stepped back and looked at what I was doing, I recognized a common thread: I was learning how to participate in this transition, and finding ways to help other people participate, too.

The technology keeps advancing. For someone still wondering where to begin, every announcement can make the mountain look bigger. You can be an accomplished person, responsible for a company, a classroom, or a family, and still feel uncertain about your own first step. It can be uncomfortable to say that out loud.

You don’t have to keep up with everything to begin.

One of the most important things I want to help people learn is recognizing the moment when AI could help. Something is confusing, unfamiliar, repetitive, or simply getting in the way—and you remember that you have another place to turn.

That recognition can become a habit. You ask, try something, question what comes back, and learn when to reach for assistance again. Your judgment develops through that experience. You remain responsible for what you accept, what you change, and what you do next.

I’ve experienced this while building software, cooking a brisket for friends, and figuring out a light repair I’d put off for years. Different situations, connected by a growing ability to approach something I didn’t already know how to do.

Helping someone else discover that capability is where I want to contribute.

For an adult beginning with AI, that might mean a useful first experience through LAUNCH, the learning program you can explore at Enjoy. For a young person, it might mean developing the confidence to investigate an unfamiliar subject while retaining their own thinking and judgment. In mentoring, it can begin with listening carefully enough to help someone recognize a possibility they hadn’t seen.

For an organization, the work extends into how people, disciplines, information, and operations connect. AI can change the pace at which ideas become working things. Making that useful takes business understanding, hands-on building, communication, and human judgment about what should happen—and why.

This site brings those interests and activities together. You’ll find my background, software you can inspect, learning experiences I’m developing, mentoring, writing, and an independent presentation about a proposed AI workforce-readiness certification initiative. Some work is built; some is being developed. I want you to be able to see both the substance and the direction.

Follow the short story, then go sideways wherever something interests you. Each path explains what I’m working on, why it matters, and gives you something deeper to explore. Along the way, you’ll also meet the person behind the work.

Your journey will be different from mine. So will your company’s, your school’s, or your family’s. If something here connects with what you’re trying to make possible, I’d enjoy a conversation.

Todd
