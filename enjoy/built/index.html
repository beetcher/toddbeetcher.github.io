How this was built

You’re looking at the lesson.

This website was built in conversation with AI, which is exactly the thing the clinic teaches. No big team, no months of work. Mostly me talking, a lot of it by voice, and some of it from my phone.

It started with a friend on a golf course

A friend told me he wanted to use AI and was embarrassed that he didn’t know how. I realized a lot of people are in that spot. So I described the idea out loud: a fun clinic, not a class, for busy people who are curious and don’t want a lecture.

What happened next

  1. I talked it through.

    I explained who it’s for, how it should feel, and what I didn’t want. Messy, out loud, stopping and starting.

  2. AI did the homework.

    It looked up what already exists for people learning AI, and found that almost nothing is aimed at busy, non-technical people in their 50s and 60s who want it to be fun.

  3. I made the decisions.

    I picked the name, said to keep the word “AI” out of the headline, and chose a bright, playful look. It never decides what the thing is for. That part is still you.

  4. We built it in pieces.

    A scrolling page of moments, a Register button, a sign-up form connected to a real database, an FAQ, and this page. Each piece was a short conversation.

  5. We tested it.

    I filled out the form myself and watched my own sign-up land in the database. That’s when it felt real.

Where it got things wrong

It wasn’t perfect, and that’s part of the lesson. It couldn’t get stock photos for the panels, so we used color for now. And it assumed the site was hosted one way when I knew it was hosted another. I corrected it, and it fixed its answer. Knowing when to push back is half the skill.

The point

I’m technical, and I still didn’t write this page line by line. I talked to an expert that lives in my pocket, made the choices, and checked the results. You can do the same with a birthday card, a brisket or a drive through a valley. When you catch yourself saying “I can do that,” that’s the whole idea.
