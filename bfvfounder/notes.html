This version begins with the intended business architecture and ownership outcome. It uses manufacturing transformation as the explanation for the opportunity, then makes the commercial and venture-creation link explicit.

What this version preserves

  • The opening paragraph is the paper’s opening paragraph, verbatim.
  • BFD has an existing manufacturing capability; enterprise-scale economics remain to be demonstrated. BFV is a proposed creation and ownership system.
  • Customer savings, BFD revenue, available profit, and BFV capital are separate. The capital bridge is conditional.
  • Both enterprise markets remain visible: transforming an existing cost structure and gaining previously unaffordable capability.
  • Both strategic clocks remain unquantified. Ongoing commercial relationships can continue beyond the initial transformation.
  • Business Design, customer evidence, human judgment, governance, and learning across ventures remain essential.

Parked for the founders

  1. Delivery evidence. Supply the factory demonstration and measured project results, with baseline, scope, quality, human effort, production cost, and maintenance context.
  2. Commercial model. Clarify the first engagement, pricing approach, customer commitments, support, and the path to sustainable profit.
  3. Capital agreement. Determine what portion of available profit could support BFV, through which mechanism, at what timing, and with what reserves and governance. Consider supplemental capital.
  4. BFV formation and rights. Clarify ownership, roles, decision authority, venture interests, and portfolio rights. No leadership or allocation is assumed here.
  5. Proof thresholds. Decide what qualifies an opportunity for testing and what earns further commitment, redirection, combination, or discontinuation.
  6. Story review. Confirm that all founders recognize the same causal chain. The chapter 6 checkpoint outputs are a proposed interpretation of QGV, rather than terms already agreed.
