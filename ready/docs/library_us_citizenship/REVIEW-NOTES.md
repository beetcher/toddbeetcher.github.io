# Review notes: U.S. citizenship library (items I am least sure about)

Everything here is `draft`. This is original study-practice material, not the official naturalization test, not affiliated with or endorsed by USCIS, and not legal or immigration advice. No claim is made that it is validated.

## First, before any real use
- **Check the current official USCIS civics test materials before using this library with real applicants.** I did not look up, and do not claim to know, which version of the official test is in effect or how it is structured. The questions here cover the same kinds of topics but are not matched to any official list, and some official items (for example anything that depends on the current officeholder, state or local government) are left out on purpose.
- I could not browse sources in this run. Facts come from my own knowledge, kept to widely agreed, stable points. A subject-matter reviewer should read every strong answer.

## Where I would look hardest (ranked)
1. **Legal framing of rights (batch 03: `rightswho_a`, `rightswho_t`, `speechlim_t`, `accused_a`).** "Many protections apply to people in the country, not only citizens" is true in general, but the details differ by right and situation. I kept the wording general and said so in the feedback. A legal reviewer may want to narrow or soften.
2. **Slavery and the founding (batch 04 `slavefound_t`).** I say the original Constitution allowed slavery to continue and avoided the word. This is standard but sensitive. Please check tone and wording.
3. **Cause of the Civil War (batch 05 `issue_g`, batch 09 `lincoln_t`).** Slavery is marked as the central issue; "states' rights" and "end slavery as the first aim" are marked `ambiguous` with editor notes because historians separate cause from stated war aim. Confirm you are comfortable with these.
4. **Forced removal of Native nations (batch 05 `removal_a`, `remnuance_t`).** Written plainly. "Trail of Tears" is used as one name for the removal of the Cherokee and other nations in the 1830s. Check the wording is respectful and precise enough.
5. **Great Depression causes (batch 06 `deprcause_t`).** Two ambiguous options (stock market crash; banking). The strong answer says "several linked causes." Check that the framing fits your view of the historiography.
6. **Judicial review (batch 02 `nuance_t`).** I say the Constitution does not use the words "judicial review" and that courts have exercised the power since early in the nation's history. Standard, but expert-level and wording-sensitive.
7. **Republic or democracy (batch 02 `demrep_t`, batch 01 `rep_dem_g`).** Both terms are accepted; some options are `ambiguous`.
8. **Voting a right or a duty (batch 09 `votingrd_t`).** The strong answer says it is a legal right and also a civic responsibility. The "only a right" answer is `ambiguous`.
9. **Civil rights (batch 06).** The Civil Rights Act of 1964 grounds listed (race, color, religion, sex, national origin) and the broad description of the movement's goals. I avoided specific cases and people other than Dr. King.

## Ambiguous choices (11 in the main files, 1 in the sample)
Each has an editor note on the connection. They are: republic and democracy wording (`demrep_t`, 2; sample `rep_dem_g`, 1), "freedom of the press" for a letter to a newspaper (`letter_g`), "states' rights" (`issue_g`), two Depression-cause options (`deprcause_t`), "passes" versus "makes" (`verb_a`), volunteering as civic life (`civic_g`, `civic_a`), "only a right" (`votingrd_t`), and the Union's first aim (`lincoln_t`).

## Stale-fact and wording risks
- **Numbers.** "Fifty states", "two senators per state", four/six/two-year terms, a two-election limit on the presidency, voting age eighteen, and the thirteen stripes are stable in the current system but are facts of law or custom that could change. I did not state the size of the House or the number of Supreme Court justices, because they are set by ordinary law.
- **Dates.** Years such as 1776, 1787, 1803, 1861 to 1865, 1941, 1964 and 1971 appear in some stems or feedback. They are settled history. The test file's stale-fact check allows historical years but blocks years from 2000 on, officeholder names, "currently", "recent", percentages and dollar amounts.
- **Holidays.** Described by meaning only (no dates or "Monday" rules) except the season of Memorial Day, Labor Day and Thanksgiving Day in the English-learner feedback.
- **National symbols.** I did not use the question of the national bird, flower or motto, because official designations have changed or are disputed. "Hail to the Chief" is described as music played for the President.
- **Place names.** I avoided the contested names of the gulf south of the Gulf Coast states and of the highest peak in North America (same reason as in the geography library).
- **Left out on purpose.** Current officeholders, party control, recent laws and rulings, state capitals and governors, and any question that depends on where the learner lives. Political parties beyond neutral definitions are not used.

## Style and fairness
- **Absolute words.** A few incorrect options contain words like "never" or "always" (for example in `hard_t`, `nuance_t`). Experienced test takers may guess these are wrong. I left them because they match real misconceptions, but you may want to soften them.
- **Uneven depth.** Expert-level sets exist for six of the eleven objectives, and mostly for tutors. Rights and responsibilities have the fewest advanced and expert sets; advanced items for symbols and geography are thin. A second pass could add more.
- **English learner level.** Short sentences and plain words are used, but a few questions contain terms that learners must know to answer (for example "ballot", "amendment"). I defined uncommon words in the stem when I could. A reading-level review by an ESL teacher would help.
- **Multiple-choice share.** About one in ten sets is select-all.
- **Answer reuse is inside each file.** A concept (for example the President's term) is asked afresh in another file rather than shared, because the import format requires every reference to resolve inside its own file.
- **Questions that overlap.** `jr_a`, `makes_e`, `enforces_e` and `courts_a` (batch 02) all ask for a branch, and `statepow_g` and `natpow_a` (batch 03) are close in topic. They test different facts but read alike. Merge or drop if you want a leaner library.
