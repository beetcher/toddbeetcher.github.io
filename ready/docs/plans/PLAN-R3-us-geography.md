## Intent
A catalog that can build geography tests for children, teens and adults. The same fact appears at different levels as different questions (a state's capital for a child; why a capital was chosen or where a river's basin lies for an adult). This is the demo of age-banded content: the `audiences` field carries the age level and `difficulty` carries depth within it.

## Audiences (use these, justify any change in TAXONOMY)
`grades_3_5` (about ages 8 to 10), `grades_6_8` (about 11 to 13), `grades_9_12` (about 14 to 18), `adult_general`, `adult_enthusiast`. Match vocabulary, sentence length and context to each band.

## Subtopics (use as topics/tags)
states and capitals; the nation's regions (name the convention you use, for example the US Census Bureau's four regions, in the question or feedback; where sources differ, mark ambiguous and say why); borders and neighbors; mountains, rivers, lakes, coasts, deserts, plains, islands and other physical features; time zones; climate and biomes; map skills (compass directions, latitude and longitude, scale, reading a described map in words); territories and the District of Columbia; national parks and well-known landmarks (stable facts only); human geography (why cities grew where they did, trade routes, land use, regional economies in general terms); geography and everyday decisions.

## Category-specific content rules
- **Evergreen only.** No populations, no rankings that change (largest or fastest-growing city), no economic statistics, no current events. Stable rankings (a state's order by land area, the longest river, the highest peak) are allowed only if you are certain; otherwise use relative or qualitative comparisons.
- Text only: describe locations in words ("bordered by the Pacific Ocean and Oregon") because there is no image support. Do not write "look at the map".
- The District of Columbia is not a state; territories are not states. Be exact.
- Where a name is contested, a boundary is disputed, or sources differ (region assignments, what counts as the "South" or "Midwest"), mark ambiguous and explain.
- Use respectful, accurate language for Indigenous place names and history; do not make jokes about regions or states.
- Younger bands: short sentences, concrete nouns, friendly feedback that explains in one or two lines. Do not use the same question wording across bands; rewrite for the band.
- Build clusters: for each important topic, create questions at several age bands so tests for each band can be assembled from the catalog.
