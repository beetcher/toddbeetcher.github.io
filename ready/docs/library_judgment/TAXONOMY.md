## Audiences
`teen_9_12`, `college_student`, `early_career_professional`, `team_lead_or_manager`, `general_adult`. Teen items use school and club settings. Manager items add other people's decisions and consequences for a team.

## Difficulty (ambiguity and competing considerations, not vocabulary)
- **introductory**: one clear consideration; plain words; the shortcut is easy to see through.
- **basic**: one main consideration and one tempting shortcut.
- **intermediate**: two real considerations that pull in different directions; a reasonable `ambiguous` choice is common.
- **advanced**: several considerations, some information missing or second-hand; the best move often reduces uncertainty before acting.
- **expert**: incomplete or conflicting information, competing duties, and consequences that appear later. Several choices look defensible; the feedback shows the weighing.

## Tag scheme
- Every record: `builder_created`, `needs_owner_review`, `ready_lib_judgment_NN`, `judgment`.
- Skill: `skill_<topic>` matching the learning objective's topic.
- Format (questions and sets): `format_scenario` (single choice, most defensible) or `format_choose_two` (multiple choice).
- Optional: `move_ask_or_delay` (sets where the strong move is to ask for more information or wait), `scenario_<name>` (shared scenario).

## Role conventions
`strong` = most defensible; `ambiguous` = reasonable with a named cost (the connection has an editor note); `incorrect` = clearly worse (weak evidence, skipped check, reasoning error). Single-choice sets have exactly one `strong`; choose-two sets have two `strong`.
