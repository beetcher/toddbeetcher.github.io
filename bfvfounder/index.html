A proposed architecture · For founder discussion

Harvest the old economy.
Build and own the new.

Black Flag intends to use its temporary advantage in AI-native software manufacturing to help established enterprises capture the financial benefits of an uncomfortable transition, then redeploy a portion of its own commercial profits—along with its manufacturing capability, Business Design, and intelligent systems—into building and owning interests in the next generation of businesses.

The harvest is the starting point.
Venture ownership is the destination.

04 / THE WINDOW

05 / How do we earn and connect the harvest?

Serve existing owners.
Earn the capital bridge.

Improve an existing capability.

Enterprises with substantial software organizations may be able to change production economics while preserving essential capability and continuity.

Make a new capability affordable.

Enterprises previously priced out of proprietary software may gain access without building a large internal production organization.

The enterprise retains its business and ownership. BFD supplies manufacturing through a commercial relationship.

Commercial judgment and the uncomfortable transition

This requires executive relationships, financial analysis, and the ability to identify enterprises with addressable costs and leadership willing to act. A proposed financial intelligence capability could extend experienced judgment.

Some roles and organizations may change. The enterprise retains authority over employment and restructuring. BFD supplies an alternative means of production.

The paper’s acquisition comparison illustrates a different economic model: an acquirer buys ownership; BFD proposes to serve incumbent owners commercially. These models have different capital requirements and risks.

07 / WHAT ENDURES

08 / The founders’ next conversation

Agree the architecture.
Prove it in sequence.

The thesis gives us a direction. Evidence and deliberate agreements determine whether the system works.

What must we prove?

  1. Repeatable manufacturing.True cost, quality, reliability, integration, and maintenance.
  2. Paid enterprise demand.Engagements that produce measurable customer benefit.
  3. Deployable commercial profit.Sustainable economics and a workable capital bridge.
  4. Productive venture creation.Evidence that qualification, validation, and development improve company-creation economics.

What must we agree?

Commercial roles and responsibilities.

The BFD-to-BFV economic arrangement.

Ownership rights, governance, and capital commitments.

Evidence thresholds and decision authority.

These are founder discussion items. No allocation, leadership appointment, or signed arrangement is implied.

We do not need to own yesterday’s companies.
We want to help build and own tomorrow’s.
